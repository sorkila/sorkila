# Research notes for Gjenfødt (night of 2026-10-27)

What was read before the deep round, and what it changed. Band: Gjenfødt. Record: Sólhvarf. Sides: Mørketid / Solverv. Imprint: Simpløyer MMXXVI.

## The lore is already in the Eddas

- **Vafþrúðnismál 47.** Odin asks where the sun will come from once Fenrir has taken her. The answer: Álfröðull (the sun) bears a daughter before the wolf catches her, and the daughter rides her mother's course when the gods have fallen. Snorri quotes it in Gylfaginning 53. This is the rebirth record in one stanza: the old product is swallowed, the new one rides the same road. Concept D1 "Sól's daughter". ([Álfröðull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lfr%C3%B6%C3%B0ull), [Vafþrúðnismál 46-50](https://www.voluspa.org/vafthrudnismal46-50.htm))
- **Völuspá 59.** The seeress sees the earth rise a second time out of the sea, green; the waterfall plunges, an eagle hunts fish over it. The unsown fields are in the stanzas next to it, check the numbering in a full edition before printing a stanza number. "Jörð ór ægi, iðjagræna" on D2 is a paraphrase, not a quotation. ([Völuspá, Larrington via heathengods](https://www.heathengods.com/voluspa/index.htm), [Open Book edition](https://books.openbookpublishers.com/10.11647/obp.0308/ch1.xhtml))
- **Líf and Lífþrasir** are in Vafþrúðnismál 45, not Völuspá: the two who hid in Hoddmímis holt (likely Yggdrasil) and come out to repeople the world. D3. ([Líf and Lífþrasir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%ADf_and_L%C3%ADf%C3%BErasir), [Hoddmímis holt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoddm%C3%ADmis_holt))

## The Norwegian canon

- **Kittelsen, Soria Moria (1900).** Twelve paintings; the one everyone knows is "Langt, langt borte så han noe lyse og glitre", the boy on the ridge and the golden castle far away. The museum's own line: a symbol of the dream of succeeding. D4 borrows the composition, never the painting. ([Nasjonalmuseet NG.M.00546](https://www.nasjonalmuseet.no/en/collection/object/NG.M.00546), [snl.no](https://snl.no/Soria_Moria_slott))
- **Munch, The Sun (1911), the Oslo aula.** 455 × 780 cm, a sunrise over the Kragerø skerries, rays painted as long strokes that run into the side panels. The sun as the source of all life, commissioned for a university. D5. ([UiO](https://www.uio.no/english/about/culture/art/aula/the-sun.html), [Munchmuseet](https://munchmuseet.no/en/the-collection/between-us-and-the-sun/))
- **Darkthrone, Transilvanian Hunger (1994).** One ultra-contrast photocopied photo of Fenriz with a candelabra, nothing else. The most copied cover in the genre because it is the cheapest. D6 swaps the candelabra for a laptop. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transilvanian_Hunger), [Noisey](https://noisey.vice.com/en_us/article/happy-20th-birthday-to-darkthrones-controversial-black-metal-masterpiece-transilvanian-hunger))
- **Szpajdel.** The Emperor logo (1991) was meant to be "iconic, unique, eye-catching, memorable" and a reaction against the bushy thicket logos; his lettering comes from Belgian Art Nouveau. D23 "the Art Nouveau logo" takes that at its word: whiplash tendrils and birch leaves, legible at arm's length. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christophe_Szpajdel), [Blazetype interview](https://blazetype.eu/blog/christophe-szpajdel), [Walker Art Center](https://walkerart.org/magazine/christophe-szpajdel-black-metal-logo-walker-art-center))

## The words

- **Solverv** is from Latin solstitium, "the sun stands still"; Norwegians also say sola snur, and **solsnu** is a listed synonym for vintersolverv. **Mørketid** is "the dark time". **Soldagen** is the north's day the sun comes back, with people on the hills to see it. **Kakelinna** is the mild spell around 21 December, named after the Christmas baking. D7 "the Ø is the sun", D8 blåtimen, D9 soldagen, D19 "SOLA SNUR". ([snl.no vintersolverv](https://snl.no/vintersolverv), [Fjord Tours](https://www.fjordtours.com/no/norge/typisk-norsk-kultur/fakta/vintersolverv), [The Local](https://www.thelocal.no/20231129/norwegian-word-of-the-day-morketid))

## The numbers

- Oslo, 59° 55′ N. Noon sun altitude about 6.6° at the December solstice and 53.5° at midsummer; about six and a half hours of daylight on 21 December. D10 the analemma, D11 the two arcs. The 6.6° is the formula 90 − (59.9 + 23.44); refraction lifts it half a degree. ([NTNU Nordic Light](https://ntnu.no/trykk/publikasjoner/NordicLightAndColour_2012/files/assets/seo/page31.html), [timeanddate Oslo](https://www.timeanddate.com/astronomy/norway/oslo))

## Print craft, for the spec

- Discharge wants 100% cotton; blends only half-discharge. One to four spot colours, the garment is a free colour. Halftones around 55 lpi, no more than a third of the mesh count; expect dot gain with discharge. A white underbase on black counts as a screen. Sample first. ([Printable Press, discharge](https://www.printablepress.com/discharge-ink-screen-printing-guide/), [Printable Press, halftones](https://www.printablepress.com/how-to-print-halftones-screen-printing/), [Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/screen-printing-basics/))
- What outlives the album: a mark fans adopt as their own (Vega's Ramones seal, the Nirvana smiley, the Dark Side prism). Which argues for one emblem that works at chest-badge size on every shirt in the line, whatever the back says. ([commonsku history](https://commonsku.com/articles/history-of-merch-band-t-shirts), [X-Press](https://xpressmag.com.au/8-of-the-most-iconic-band-t-shirts/))

## What it changed

1. A chest emblem that can carry the line: the Ø-as-sun wordmark (D7) or the Art Nouveau logo (D23).
2. Three "true" pictures from the Eddas (wolf and daughter, the green earth, the two who stayed) instead of generic grim.
3. Typeset objects that need no illustration to argue about: liner notes, centre label, analemma, the broadsheet.
4. Spec: 100% cotton garment-dyed black, discharge bone + one gold, halftone art at 55 lpi, order one run.
